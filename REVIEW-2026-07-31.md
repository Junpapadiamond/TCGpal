# TCGlens Review — 31 Jul 2026

Live review of https://tcgpal.vercel.app/ in Chrome. Flows tested: landing → search → version confirm → result, deep-link replay, ambiguous query (`Pikachu`), no-match query, footer/nav links, console.

---

## The one-sentence version

The thinking behind this product is better than almost anything in the category — but three broken things (a warning that fires 100% of the time, a trust page that doesn't exist, and results that don't persist) are actively undermining the trust the whole product is selling.

---

## Strengths — protect these

These are real differentiators. Don't refactor them away.

1. **Parse chips.** Typing `Charizard ex 199/165` surfaced `Pokémon` / `Charizard ex` / `#199/165` before I committed. No competitor does this. It converts a black-box search into a visible contract. Best thing on the site.
2. **Version gate before pricing.** Pausing at "Choose your Pikachu" is the correct product instinct. Wrong-print pricing is *the* hard problem in TCG and most tools paper over it.
3. **Landed cost as the headline number.** Item + shipping + tax, not item price. This matches how buyers actually get burned — on eBay, fees and tax apply to the shipping line too, so a "$380 card" is routinely $430 out the door.
4. **"Why not the cheapest."** Explaining the counterfactual ("saves $12.22 but 2 vs 15 photos and a weaker seller record") is rare and genuinely persuasive.
5. **Honest hedging in copy.** "seller-stated Near Mint," "the return policy was not verified," "condition is still the seller's claim." This is what an evidence product should sound like.
6. **Optimize-for toggles** (Best value / Cheapest / Safest / Documented). Correctly models that "best" is a preference, not a fact.
7. **Visual identity.** Serif display + warm off-white + deep teal reads calm and considered — not like a scraper. Bilingual EN/中文 out of the gate is a real asset for this market.

## P0 — Fix this week

### 1. The "above reference" badge is structurally broken
All 13 listings showed red `+11%` to `+14% above reference`. Market shown as $377.63; landed costs $418–$430.

You are comparing **landed cost** (item + ship + tax) against a **market price that excludes ship + tax**. The gap *is* the shipping and tax. The badge can never be green — it is mathematically guaranteed to be red on every listing, forever.

A warning that fires 100% of the time is not a warning; it's decoration that trains users to ignore your signals.

Also: the red badge sits directly beside a green box saying "Reasonable to buy." You are contradicting yourself inside one card.

**Fix:** compare landed-to-landed (add estimated ship + tax to the reference), or relabel to `Item price vs. market: +2%` and show the breakdown (`$390 card + $28 ship & tax`). Green/amber/red should each be reachable.

### 2. Method / Data sources / What we don't do are dead links
All three footer links and the nav "Method" link point to `/method`, which redirects to `/#method` — an anchor that doesn't exist. You land back on the homepage.

Your entire positioning is transparency and audit trail. The transparency page is a 404. This is the cheapest, highest-leverage fix on the list.

**Fix:** ship a real `/method` page. Where prices come from, what "reference" means, how landed cost is computed, what "37 excluded" excluded and why, what you deliberately don't do (no grading opinion, no authentication, no counterfeit detection). Also gives you your first real SEO surface.

### 3. Results don't persist
Deep-linking `?query=...&step=result&card=sv3pt5-199` re-ran the entire pipeline from scratch (~20–25s) and showed the wrong loading copy — "Reading your listing," which belongs to the paste-a-link flow.

Consequences: **Share receipt** produces a link that doesn't reproduce the receipt; you pay eBay API cost twice for identical queries; zero cacheable/indexable pages.

**Fix:** persist each result under a stable ID, serve it as a static snapshot with "as of Jul 30, 5:54 PM" plus a Refresh button. Turns the receipt from a nice idea into an actual artifact.

### 4. Card images fail to load
In the Pikachu version picker, all four "most likely matches" thumbnails were still blank white boxes after 9 seconds; several set-group images never loaded. Hero card images blanked entirely on one reload.

For a product whose core job is *"pick the right print,"* blank images make the picker unusable.

**Fix:** proxy + cache images on your own CDN, set explicit aspect ratios so layout doesn't shift, and add a text/set-symbol fallback so a failed image still shows set + number + rarity.

### 5. 20–25s to first result, with a stalled progress UI
The 4-step panel ("Checking connected listing sources / Verifying version and condition fit / Calculating comparable total / Preparing the recommendation") is well-designed — but all four spun simultaneously and none ever ticked to complete.

**Fix:** either actually resolve them in sequence, or stream the top pick as soon as it's known and backfill the comparison list. Perceived speed matters more than real speed here.

## P1 — Product

### 6. You're answering the smaller question
All 13 listings sat in a $418–$430 band. That's a **3% spread**. Winning the pick inside that band is worth ~$12.

The useful headline was sitting in your own footer: *every copy of this card is currently 11–14% above market.* That's the "when to pass" you promise on the homepage and then don't deliver.

**Reframe the result page:**
> **Market position: expensive right now.** All 13 available copies are 11–14% above the $377 market. If you can wait, wait. If you can't — here's the best of them.

That's a reason to come back. "Which of these 13 near-identical listings" is not.

### 7. "Medium confidence" on every single version card
Same failure as #1 — a label that never varies. Either make it vary meaningfully or delete it. Right now it just adds low-grade anxiety to a decision screen.

### 8. Version picker shows no prices
Choosing among 50 Pikachus, the single most useful disambiguator is *"this one's $2, that one's $400."* You show rarity and set but no value. Add a ballpark price to each version card and most users pick correctly in one second.

### 9. "Showing 50 of 50 versions" is a cap dressed as completeness
Pikachu has hundreds of printings. Say `50 of 300+`, and add a free-text filter — Set and Rarity dropdowns alone won't cut a 300-item list.

### 10. No memory, so no reason to return
No saved searches, no watchlist, no alerts. Card buying is a repeat behavior with a waiting period. You already ask *"Did this evidence change what you would do?" → Yes / Not yet.* "Not yet" is a **subscription intent signal** and you're throwing it away.

**Fix:** "Not yet" → "Want an email when a Near Mint copy drops below $400 landed?" That's your retention loop, and it's ~a day of work.

### 11. eBay only
The homepage says "compares live listings." One source isn't a comparison. The 2026 standard trio is TCGplayer + eBay sold + Cardmarket — [Eyevo](https://eyevotcg.com/blog/best-pokemon-card-scanner-apps-2026/) and [Lens App](https://lensapp.io/card-scanner/pokemon-card-price-checker/) both do all three. Adding TCGplayer alone would meaningfully widen your spread and make the "best value" claim defensible.

### 12. No photo/scan entry
Every consumer competitor in 2026 leads with camera scan — CollX, Ludex, [Eyevo](https://eyevotcg.com/blog/best-pokemon-card-scanner-apps-2026/), [DittoDex](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ptcgtracker&hl=en_US), [Brickify](https://www.brickify.com/blog/best-pokemon-card-scanner-apps-2026). Typing `Charizard ex 199/165` assumes the user already knows the collector number — that's the expert case. Your parse chips are the perfect landing surface for a scan result: photo → chips → confirm.

## P2 — Design

13. **Landing page is one screen with nothing below the fold.** A first-time visitor sees a form and cannot picture the output. Put a real result card below the fold — the Charizard one, verbatim. *Show* the "why not the cheapest" reasoning. Highest-converting single change you can make.
14. **Result card has 8 labeled sections at similar visual weight** (OUR PICK / BEST VALUE / 1 of 13 / Confirmed print / ACTION / WHY IT STANDS OUT / WHAT TO KNOW / WHY NOT THE CHEAPEST). Collapse to: verdict → price + market position → one reason → "show full reasoning."
15. **Listing titles are truncated** (`...Scarlet & Violet 151 Speci...`). The seller's own wording is exactly the evidence a careful buyer scrutinizes — it's where fake/wrong-version tells show up. Show it in full.
16. **No price distribution visual.** 13 listings, one number each. A single dot plot — market price marked, your pick highlighted — would communicate the entire comparison at a glance and make the "everything is overpriced" story self-evident.
17. **`Ask` links in comparison rows are unlabeled.** Unclear affordance; looks like a stray word.
18. **Empty state is a dead end.** "No catalog match was found" appears under a header still reading "Choose your card / Version confirmation required" — contradictory. No button back to search. Add a recovery CTA and 2–3 suggested corrections.
19. **Mobile unverified.** Window resize didn't take effect in my session, so I couldn't confirm. Check it yourself — the result card is dense two-column, and card buying frequently happens on a phone at a shop or show.

## Brand

- **Buy the domain.** `tcgpal.vercel.app` while the brand is TCGlens is a double credibility hit: mismatched name, and `.vercel.app` reads "side project." ~$12 for a large trust gain on a product that sells trust.
- **"think before you buy"** (the wordmark subtitle) is a strong line and it's set at ~6px where nobody can read it. Promote it.

---

## Suggested order

**Week 1 — trust.** Method page · fix the reference baseline · buy the domain · cache results.
**Week 2 — core loop.** Image proxy/fallback · stream results · prices in version picker · delete non-varying signals.
**Week 3 — value.** Lead with market position · watchlist + price alert on "Not yet" · real result card on the landing page.

Everything in Week 1 is small and mechanical. If you're running low on energy, that week is the right place to start — it's the highest trust-per-hour work on the list, and none of it requires a design decision.
