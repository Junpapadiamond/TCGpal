## Decision and test boundary

The founder wants working cross-market offers and explicitly permits alternatives to Whatnot and Mercari. The underlying goal is broader purchase options for an already-selected card. Raising an exhausted scraper quota is only one possible mechanism.

The strongest case for renewing Whatnot is its historical success and existing integration. Two serious objections are that the 20-start lifetime ceiling guarantees another shutdown, and the failed Mercari transport cannot be fixed by buying credits. Neither historical data nor a configured token proves current acquisition or complete checkout costs.

Choose the documented merchant-owned Stomping Grounds Shopify Catalog API for an additional price-display source. Ten read-only calls across five cards in both games returned repeatable variant offers without a paid provider. Two serious limitations remain: merchant maintenance can make inventory inaccurate, and shipping/mandatory fees are absent. Its search also returns irrelevant numbers and mixed raw/graded or available/unavailable variants. These are deterministic filtering and visible-uncertainty requirements, not permission to invent facts.

The bounded test succeeds when the confirmed number survives filtering, individual raw USD variants have explicit available claims and condition prices, the same acquisition can run in the deployed environment, and no incomplete-cost offer becomes a winner. Kill on API access denial, unhandled schema drift, unsupported monetary units, absent availability, or a fabricated recommendation. Five cards are a small operational sample, not a claim of universal exact-print precision or inventory accuracy. Live comparison must still abstain from ranking the source while charges are unknown.

### Rate-limit and operator recovery

Production acquisition requires Redis. A shared, owner-checked in-flight latch serializes requests; HTTP 429 publishes the greatest observed `Retry-After` deadline atomically before releasing it. Failed reads/writes stop acquisition. A process crash or failed cooldown publication intentionally leaves the latch without an expiry, so a deployment cannot silently bypass a possible merchant block.

If the source reports an operator pause, first disable `STOMPING_GROUNDS_ENABLED` and inspect the affected request/logs. The two keys are generated by `toCacheKey("stomping-grounds", "in-flight")` and `toCacheKey("stomping-grounds", "cooldown")` in `src/lib/ops/cache.ts`; they are separate from the paid pilot counter. Confirm no request remains active and honor the full merchant retry deadline. If that deadline is unknown or unrepresentable, leave acquisition disabled pending merchant/operator review. Only after the access restriction is resolved may an operator remove the stale in-flight key and re-enable the source. Never delete an active cooldown, reset the paid counter, or use another transport to get around a block.

## Whatnot budget repair

`tcglens:apify-pilot:2026-09-14:reserved-runs` remains the immutable shared counter. Default maximum remains 20. An atomic Redis script checks before incrementing, fixing the bug where every rejected search inflated the count.

The optional approval pair is `APIFY_PILOT_APPROVED_MAX_RUNS` (cumulative integer 21–10,000) plus `APIFY_PILOT_APPROVAL_EXPIRES_AT` (canonical UTC ISO timestamp). It extends only Whatnot. Partial, invalid or expired approval fails closed; Redis also checks expiry at reservation time. A lost response is never refunded/retried. Per-run pricing checks, three-row caps and $0.03/$0.05 maximum charges remain unchanged.

Before any renewal, read the existing counter and obtain approval for an explicit incremental number of starts, maximum dollar exposure and expiry; derive the cumulative ceiling from that reading. Do not assume it is exactly 20: historical rejected calls inflated it. No renewal values, subscription, paid test or counter mutation were authorized during this implementation. Existing Apify Console showed $0.01/$5.00 usage; that is account credit visibility, not new spending authorization.

Mercari stays disabled after its failed HTTP 403/zero-row test. Its access problem is unresolved.
