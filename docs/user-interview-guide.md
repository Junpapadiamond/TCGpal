# User interview guide (5–10 buyers)

Purpose: test the #1 risk — **will someone in a FOMO moment actually pause to check?** — and understand the real buying decision well enough to lock ICP, wedge, and aha. This is NOT a product pitch. Do not demo TCGpal until the very end (if at all).

## Mom Test rules (read before every call)
- Ask about their **life and past behavior**, never about your idea.
- Talk about the **specific last time**, not hypotheticals ("would you…" answers are worthless).
- **Don't pitch. Don't lead.** No "wouldn't it be great if…".
- Compliments are not data. Dig for concrete actions, money, time, and effort already spent.
- You should be listening ~80% of the time.

## Who to recruit
- Bought a TCG card in the last 30 days. Bonus: someone who's regretted/overpaid.
- Deliberately mix **collectors** and **flippers/small sellers** — you're hunting for which one has the sharper pain (that becomes your ICP).
- Recruit from the people you helped in communities (warm, relevant). Offer a small thank-you (gift card / store credit).

## Script (~30 min)

**Warm-up**
- What do you collect/buy, and how long have you been at it?
- Roughly how often do you buy, and where?

**The last purchase (concrete story — spend the most time here)**
- Walk me through the last card you bought. Start from how you found it.
- How did you decide it was the one? How long did the decision take?
- What, if anything, did you check before buying? (price? version? condition? seller?)
- How did you feel right after? A week later?

**Regret probe**
- Tell me about a purchase you regretted or felt you overpaid on. What happened?
- Looking back, what would have changed that decision? (let them answer — don't suggest)

**The FOMO moment (the key test)**
- Tell me about the last time you felt "buy now or I'll miss it." What did you do?
- In that moment, did you check anything, or just buy? Why?
- Have you ever stopped yourself mid-purchase? What made you stop? (← listen hard: does self-pausing behavior exist naturally?)

**Current behavior / existing tools**
- Today, how do you check if a price is fair? Authenticity? The exact version?
- What tools/sites/people do you use? (reveals the real job-to-be-done and competitors)

**Channels**
- Where do you hang out for cards? Where do you ask "should I buy this?" or get info?

**Wrap**
- Anything about buying cards that I should have asked but didn't?
- Could I follow up, and do you know 1–2 others I should talk to?

## ❌ Don't ask (leading / hypothetical) → ✅ ask instead
- ❌ "Would you use a tool that helps you pause before buying?" → ✅ "Have you ever stopped yourself mid-purchase? How?"
- ❌ "Don't you hate overpaying?" → ✅ "Tell me about the last time you felt you overpaid."
- ❌ "Would a price-vs-sold check be useful?" → ✅ "How do you check if a price is fair today?"

## What to listen for (signals)
- **Problem real?** Do they already spend time/money/effort checking? Real pain leaves a trail.
- **Friction tolerated?** Any evidence they self-pause naturally. If nobody ever stops themselves, your core bet is in danger — that's the most important finding.
- **ICP signal** — which group (collector vs flipper) describes a sharper, more frequent pain?
- **Aha candidates** — what check do they wish was faster/easier (price-vs-comp? fake cert? version)?
- **Channels** — where they already ask the buy question = your distribution beachhead.

## After 5 interviews
Look for patterns, not anecdotes. Decide: is the friction assumption supported? Which ICP? Which aha? Then commit, and re-run if the picture is still murky.
