## Content patterns

### <a id="1"></a>1. Undue emphasis on significance, legacy, broader trends
**Watch:** stands/serves as, is a testament/reminder, vital/significant/crucial/pivotal/key role/moment, underscores/highlights its importance, reflects broader, symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting.
> Before: The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain.
> After: The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was established in 1989 to collect and publish regional statistics independently.

### <a id="2"></a>2. Undue emphasis on notability / media coverage
**Watch:** independent coverage, local/regional/national media outlets, written by a leading expert, active social media presence.
> Before: Her views have been cited in The New York Times, BBC, Financial Times, and The Hindu. She maintains an active social media presence with over 500,000 followers.
> After: In a 2024 New York Times interview, she argued that AI regulation should focus on outcomes rather than methods.

### <a id="3"></a>3. Superficial analysis with -ing endings
**Watch:** highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting/symbolizing, contributing to, cultivating/fostering, encompassing, showcasing.
> Before: The temple's color palette resonates with the region's natural beauty, symbolizing Texas bluebonnets, the Gulf of Mexico, and diverse Texan landscapes.
> After: The temple uses blue, green, and gold colors. The architect said these reference local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast.

### <a id="4"></a>4. Promotional / advertisement-like language
**Watch:** boasts a, vibrant, rich, profound, enhancing its, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, breathtaking, must-visit, stunning.
> Before: Nestled within the breathtaking region of Gonder in Ethiopia, Alamata Raya Kobo stands as a vibrant town with rich cultural heritage.
> After: Alamata Raya Kobo is a town in the Gonder region of Ethiopia, known for its weekly market and 18th-century church.

### <a id="5"></a>5. Vague attribution / weasel words
**Watch:** industry reports, observers have cited, experts argue, some critics argue, several sources/publications.
> Before: Due to its unique characteristics, the Haolai River is of interest to researchers. Experts believe it plays a crucial role in the ecosystem.
> After: The Haolai River supports several endemic fish species, according to a 2019 survey by the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

### <a id="6"></a>6. Outline-like "Challenges and Future Prospects" sections
**Watch:** despite its challenges, despite these challenges, Challenges and Legacy, Future Outlook.
> Before: Despite its industrial prosperity, Korattur faces challenges typical of urban areas, including traffic congestion and water scarcity.
> After: Traffic congestion increased after 2015 when three new IT parks opened. The municipal corporation began a drainage project in 2022.

## Language & grammar patterns

### <a id="7"></a>7. Overused "AI vocabulary"
**High-frequency:** additionally, align with, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight, interplay, intricate, key, landscape, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant.
> Before: Additionally, camel meat is distinctive in Somali cuisine. An enduring testament to Italian colonial influence is pasta's widespread adoption.
> After: Somali cuisine also includes camel meat, considered a delicacy. Pasta, introduced during Italian colonization, remains common.

### <a id="8"></a>8. Copula avoidance (dodging "is/are")
**Watch:** serves as/stands as/marks/represents, boasts/features/offers.
> Before: Gallery 825 serves as LAAA's exhibition space. The gallery features four separate spaces and boasts over 3,000 square feet.
> After: Gallery 825 is LAAA's exhibition space. The gallery has four rooms totaling 3,000 square feet.

### <a id="9"></a>9. Negative parallelism & tailing negations
**Watch:** "not only…but…", "it's not just…it's…", fragments like "no guessing" tacked on.
> Before: It's not just about the beat; it's part of the aggression. It's not merely a song, it's a statement.
> After: The heavy beat adds to the aggressive tone.
> Before: The options come from the selected item, no guessing.
> After: The options come from the selected item without forcing the user to guess.

### <a id="10"></a>10. Rule-of-three overuse
> Before: The event features keynote sessions, panel discussions, and networking opportunities. Attendees can expect innovation, inspiration, and insights.
> After: The event includes talks and panels. There's also time for informal networking between sessions.

### <a id="11"></a>11. Elegant variation (synonym cycling)
> Before: The protagonist faces many challenges. The main character must overcome obstacles. The central figure eventually triumphs.
> After: The protagonist faces many challenges but eventually triumphs and returns home.

### <a id="12"></a>12. False ranges
**Watch:** "from X to Y" where X and Y aren't on a real scale.
> Before: Our journey spans from the singularity of the Big Bang to the grand cosmic web, from star birth to dark matter theories.
> After: The book covers the Big Bang, star formation, and current theories about dark matter.

### <a id="13"></a>13. Passive voice & subjectless fragments
> Before: No configuration file needed. The results are preserved automatically.
> After: You do not need a configuration file. The system preserves the results automatically.

## Style patterns

### <a id="14"></a>14. Em/en dashes — cut them
**Rule:** the final rewrite contains zero em dashes (—) or en dashes (–). Replace with period, comma, colon, parentheses, or restructure.
> Before: The term is promoted by Dutch institutions—not by the people themselves. You don't say "Netherlands, Europe"—yet this continues—even officially.
> After: The term is promoted by Dutch institutions, not by the people themselves. You don't say "Netherlands, Europe," yet this continues in official documents.

### <a id="15"></a>15. Boldface overuse
> Before: It blends **OKRs**, **KPIs**, and tools like the **Business Model Canvas** and **Balanced Scorecard**.
> After: It blends OKRs, KPIs, and tools like the Business Model Canvas and Balanced Scorecard.

### <a id="16"></a>16. Inline-header vertical lists
> Before:
> - **User Experience:** The user experience has been significantly improved with a new interface.
> - **Performance:** Performance has been enhanced through optimized algorithms.
>
> After: The update improves the interface, speeds up load times through optimized algorithms, and adds end-to-end encryption.

### <a id="17"></a>17. Title Case in headings
> Before: ## Strategic Negotiations And Global Partnerships
> After: ## Strategic negotiations and global partnerships

### <a id="18"></a>18. Emojis as decoration
> Before: 🚀 **Launch Phase:** The product launches in Q3 · 💡 **Key Insight:** Users prefer simplicity
> After: The product launches in Q3. User research showed a preference for simplicity.

### <a id="19"></a>19. Curly quotation marks
**Problem:** ChatGPT uses curly quotes (“ ”) instead of straight quotes ("..."). Normalize to straight quotes.
> Before: He said “the project is on track” but others disagreed.
> After: He said "the project is on track" but others disagreed.

## Communication patterns

### <a id="20"></a>20. Collaborative chat artifacts
**Watch:** I hope this helps, Of course!, Certainly!, You're absolutely right!, Would you like, Want me to, Let me know.
> Before: Here is an overview of the French Revolution. I hope this helps! Let me know if you'd like me to expand on any section.
> After: The French Revolution began in 1789 when financial crisis and food shortages led to widespread unrest.

### <a id="21"></a>21. Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers & speculative gap-filling
**Watch:** as of [date], up to my last training update, while specific details are limited, based on available information, likely [grew up/studied/began].
> Before: While specific details about the company's founding are not extensively documented, it appears to have been established in the 1990s.
> After: The company was founded in 1994, according to its registration documents.
> Before: Information about her early life is not publicly available. She likely grew up in a middle-class household, which shaped her interest in education reform.
> After: Her early life is not documented in the available sources.

### <a id="22"></a>22. Sycophantic / servile tone
> Before: Great question! You're absolutely right. That's an excellent point about the economic factors.
> After: The economic factors you mentioned are relevant here.

## Filler & hedging

### <a id="23"></a>23. Filler phrases
- "In order to achieve this goal" → "To achieve this"
- "Due to the fact that it was raining" → "Because it was raining"
- "At this point in time" → "Now"
- "The system has the ability to process" → "The system can process"
- "It is important to note that the data shows" → "The data shows"

### <a id="24"></a>24. Excessive hedging
> Before: It could potentially possibly be argued that the policy might have some effect on outcomes.
> After: The policy may affect outcomes.

### <a id="25"></a>25. Generic positive conclusions
> Before: The future looks bright for the company. Exciting times lie ahead as they continue their journey toward excellence.
> After: The company plans to open two more locations next year.

## Texture — subtler tells

### <a id="26"></a>26. Hyphenated word-pair overuse
**Watch:** third-party, cross-functional, client-facing, data-driven, decision-making, well-known, high-quality, real-time, long-term, end-to-end. Drop the hyphen in predicate position (after the noun), as humans do.
> Before: The team is cross-functional and high-quality.
> After: The team is cross functional and high quality.

### <a id="27"></a>27. Persuasive-authority tropes
**Watch:** the real question is, at its core, in reality, what really matters, fundamentally, the deeper issue, the heart of the matter.
> Before: The real question is whether teams can adapt. What really matters is organizational readiness.
> After: The question is whether teams can adapt. That mostly depends on whether the organization is ready to change.

### <a id="28"></a>28. Signposting & announcements
**Watch:** let's dive in, let's explore, let's break this down, here's what you need to know, without further ado.
> Before: Let's dive into how caching works in Next.js. Here's what you need to know.
> After: Next.js caches data at multiple layers: request memoization, the data cache, and the router cache.

### <a id="29"></a>29. Fragmented headers
**Problem:** a heading followed by a one-line generic restatement before the real content.
> Before: ## Performance / Speed matters. / When users hit a slow page, they leave.
> After: ## Performance / When users hit a slow page, they leave.

### <a id="30"></a>30. Diff-anchored writing
**Problem:** describing a thing by narrating the change rather than what it is (common in docs/comments).
> Before: This function was added to replace the previous approach of iterating through all items, which caused O(n²) performance.
> After: This function uses a hash map for O(1) lookups, avoiding the O(n²) cost of naive iteration.

### <a id="31"></a>31. Manufactured punchlines & staccato drama
**Problem:** every sentence lands like a quotable closer; short fragments stacked for false drama.
> Before: Then AlphaEvolve arrived. It had no preference for symmetry. No aesthetic prior. No nostalgia. The old rules were gone.
> After: AlphaEvolve changed the search because it did not favor symmetry or human-looking designs, making older assumptions less useful.

### <a id="32"></a>32. Aphorism formulas
**Watch:** "X is the Y of Z", "X becomes a trap", "X is not a tool but a mirror", "the language of", "the currency of".
> Before: Symmetry is the language of trust. Efficiency becomes a trap when teams forget the human layer.
> After: Symmetric layouts often feel more predictable to users. Teams can over-optimize workflows and miss how people actually use them.

### <a id="33"></a>33. Conversational rhetorical openers
**Watch:** Honestly?, Look, Here's the thing, The thing is, Let's be honest, Real talk (as standalone hooks).
> Before: Is it worth the price? Honestly? It depends on how often you'll use it.
> After: Whether it's worth the price depends on how often you'll use it.
